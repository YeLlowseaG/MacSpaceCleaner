FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Direct answers about cleanup boundaries, privacy, safety and cache behavior.

Cleanup scope

What can it clean?

It can identify common app caches and logs, developer caches from npm, pnpm, bun, Homebrew and Playwright, plus old Xcode simulator runtimes and shared caches.

Does it delete personal files?

No. Downloads, iCloud Drive, WeChat chat data and WPS backups are view-only and excluded from one-click cleanup. Optional items are off by default.

Privacy and safety

Is scan data uploaded?

No. Scanning happens locally. File paths, scan results and cleanup history are not sent to the developer's servers.

Is Full Disk Access required?

No. Normal user caches can be scanned and cleaned without it. macOS may separately ask for access to Downloads, iCloud Drive or another protected folder. If you decline, that folder is omitted while other features continue to work. Full Disk Access is optional for users who want to inspect more protected app data.

What other system permissions are requested?

The app does not need Accessibility, Screen Recording, Camera, Microphone, Contacts, Photos, Location or Automation access. It does not install a background service or system extension.

Is the app notarized by Apple?

The current version is Developer ID signed and has passed Apple notarization and Gatekeeper verification.

Xcode simulators

Will it delete my newest simulator?

No. The app identifies runtimes by platform and automatically keeps the newest version for each platform. Only older runtimes are offered as optional cleanup.

Cache behavior

Why do caches come back after cleaning?

Caches are temporary data created to make apps faster. Some are naturally rebuilt when you use the related app again.

Why is an app not shown?

The app only shows discovered caches that match its rules. An app may have no cache, a small cache, a protected location or a path not yet covered by the rules.

Do I need an administrator password?

Normal user caches usually do not need one. Protected system directories are not included in generic one-click cleanup.
